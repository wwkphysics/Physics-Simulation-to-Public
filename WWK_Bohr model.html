Teaching guide and model assumptions
  • Absorb a photon to move to a higher energy level. Emit a photon to move to a lower level.
  • Prepare n = 3, select emission to n = 2, and compare with absorption from n = 2 to n = 3.
  • Use a non-matching photon energy to test whether absorption occurs.
  • Ionisation requires at least the binding energy. Any excess becomes the electron's kinetic energy, neglecting nuclear recoil.

This simplified Bohr model applies to hydrogen. It displays n = 1–6; higher bound levels are not simulated. Orbital motion, jump timing and photon wave packets are illustrative. Orbit radii are spaced for clarity: physical Bohr radii increase as n². Bound-state matches use energies rounded to 0.01 eV as an input convenience, not a physical absorption linewidth. The ionisation threshold uses the unrounded binding energy. Spontaneous lifetimes, transition probabilities, selection rules and automatic cascades are not modelled. Brackett and Pfund names are extension information.

Approximate constants: E₁ = −13.6 eV, hc = 1240 eV nm. Reference: OpenStax — Bohr's model of hydrogen.
