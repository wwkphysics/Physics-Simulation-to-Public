Teaching guide
  • Use a single pulse first. Compare its displacement before and after reflection at a fixed end and at a free end.
  • Switch to Stationary wave. Follow the reflected wave back along the string, then show the established wave.
  • Hide or separate the component waves. At each point, add the red and blue displacements to obtain the resultant.
  • Reveal nodes and antinodes. Adjacent nodes are λ/2 apart; a node and its nearest antinode are λ/4 apart.
  • Track a point. The point moves transversely, while the component wave patterns travel along the string.
Physics and model assumptions

Equal-amplitude incident and reflected waves travel at the same speed. At a fixed end, reflection introduces a phase change of π (180°), giving a node. At a free end, there is no phase change, giving an antinode. In the overlap region, continuous waves of equal frequency form a stationary (standing) wave.

The ideal string has a uniform wave speed and no damping or dispersion. The incoming wave is supplied from outside the left edge. Waves returning through that edge leave without a second reflection; this model has one reflecting boundary and does not demonstrate resonance between two reflecting ends. The diagram is vertically enlarged.

For a source displacement s(t), yᵢ(x,t) = s(t − x/v) and yᵣ(x,t) = r s(t − (2L − x)/v), where r = −1 for a fixed end and r = +1 for a free end. The resultant is y = yᵢ + yᵣ. The source is zero before t = 0.
