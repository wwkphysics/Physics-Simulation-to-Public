Extension · Why does the current fall near the threshold?

Some electrons in the metal need more energy to escape than others. The work function is the minimum energy needed. If each photon carries only a little more than this minimum, fewer of the electrons that absorb photons can escape.

Set the collector voltage high enough to reach saturation. Keep the light intensity fixed and increase the wavelength towards the threshold. More photons arrive each second because each carries less energy. Close to the threshold, the chance that a photon releases an electron falls fast enough for the saturation current to decrease.

How to use this simulation
  • Keep the light settings fixed. Increase the collector voltage from zero until the current levels off.
  • Keep the light intensity fixed and vary the wavelength. How does the current approach zero?
  • Try a negative collector voltage. When does the current fall to zero?

Current is shown in arbitrary units. Light intensity is the power per unit area reaching the target, expressed as a percentage of the model maximum. V = potential of collector − potential of target. UV and IR use false colours. Apparatus dimensions are not to scale.

Teaching guide and model assumptions
  • At fixed light intensity, change the wavelength towards the threshold and compare the saturation currents.
  • At fixed light settings, increase the collector voltage and watch how many emitted electrons reach the collector.
  • Show energy and apply a retarding voltage to find the stopping potential.

Light intensity is the power per unit area reaching the target. The simulation uses the same illustrative energy scale of 0.06 eV for every metal to set how gradually the emission probability falls near the threshold. The width of the drop in a measured curve depends on the photocathode and its surface condition.

Electrons leave the target at different angles with a uniform spread of initial kinetic energies from zero to Kmax. A higher positive collector voltage accelerates them across the gap, so they drift less sideways and more reach the collector. The current levels off when all emitted electrons are collected.

V is the collector potential minus the target potential. The model uses a uniform electric field and shows a steady current. Animation is slowed down. Particle sizes and apparatus dimensions are not to scale. UV and IR use false colours. The model uses c ≈ 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ and hc ≈ 1240 eV nm.
